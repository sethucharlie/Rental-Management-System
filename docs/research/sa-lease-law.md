# South African residential lease law: notes for this app

Researched 26 September 2026. Written for a small landlord (8 flats and one house on one property, rent about R1 500 a month) who runs leases through this web app.

**This is research, not legal advice.** See the note at the end.

## How to read this

- Each claim cites the Act or rule, the section, and a link to the text I read.
- I read the Acts and rules themselves (gov.za, justice.gov.za, saflii.org). Law firm blogs only pointed me to them.
- **Unsure** marks a point where the law is not settled or I could not confirm it from a primary source.
- "RHA" = Rental Housing Act 50 of 1999. "CPA" = Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008. "Reg 5" = regulation 5 of the CPA Regulations (GN R293, 1 April 2011). "ECTA" = Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002.

## 0. Which laws apply

### 0.1 Rental Housing Act 50 of 1999 and its amendments

- The RHA came into force on 1 August 2000 (Proc 42, GG 21396, 28 July 2000). Source: SAFLII consolidated text header, <https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/rha1999171/>.
- **Rental Housing Amendment Act 43 of 2007 is in force** from 13 May 2008. It has no start-date clause, so it took effect when published (GG 31051, 13 May 2008). It changed s5(3)(b) (receipts), s5(3)(d) (deposit interest) and added s5(3)(p) (costs only on proof of real spending). Text: <https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/gg31051nn539pg1-6.pdf>. gov.za lists "Commencement 13 May 2008" on <https://www.gov.za/documents/rental-housing-amendment-act>.
- **Rental Housing Amendment Act 35 of 2014 is NOT in force.**
  - Section 22 says it "comes into operation on a date determined by the President by Proclamation in the Gazette". Section 21 adds that new duties start six months after that date. Text: <https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201411/38184act35of2014rentalhousingamendment5nov2014.pdf>.
  - gov.za's page for the Act says "Commencement to be proclaimed in a Government Gazette": <https://www.gov.za/documents/rental-housing-amendment-act-0>.
  - SAFLII's consolidated RHA, "last checked 5 December 2025", marks every 2014 change "Uncommenced ... with effect from date to be proclaimed": <https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/rha1999171/>.
  - I searched for a start-date proclamation and found none. **Unsure:** I cannot prove a negative. A proclamation between December 2025 and today (September 2026) could have been missed. Check the Government Gazette before relying on this.
  - What the 2014 Act would change if it starts: leases must always be in writing (new s5(1)); the deposit rules move into new s4A and s4B, mostly with the same deadlines; the lease must set out the rights in s4A and s4B (new s5(6)(fA)); the deposit is kept out of the landlord's estate on death or insolvency (new s4B(1)(d)).
- Draft national unfair-practice rules were published for comment in 2008 (GN 340, GG 30863, <https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/30863340.pdf>) and 2022 (GN 1913, GG 46063, <https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202203/46063gon1913.pdf>). Both are marked as drafts. I found no final version.
- **Provincial unfair-practice rules.** Under RHA s15(1)(f), a province may make rules on "unfair practices". They can cover deposits, house rules, receipts and more. **The property is in the Eastern Cape. I could not find Eastern Cape rules** (see "Eastern Cape rules" below). I first read the Gauteng rules as an example (Unfair Practices Regulations, 2001, copy on the Gauteng government site: <https://cmbinary.gauteng.gov.za/Media?path=housing/Documents/Gauteng+Rental+Tribunal/Department+of+Human+Settlements+Unfair++Practices+Regulations+2001.pdf&Item=190&Type=Documents&Location=/housing>). **Gauteng's rules do not apply to this property.** They stay in this note, marked "[Gauteng, does not apply]", only to show the kind of rule a province can make.

### 0.2 Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008: does it cover this landlord?

Text: <https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/321864670.pdf> (GG 32186, 29 April 2009).

- The CPA covers "every transaction occurring within the Republic" unless exempt (s5(1)(a)).
- A lease can be a CPA "service": the definition includes "access to or use of any premises or other property in terms of a rental" (s1, "service" (e)(v)).
- But "rental" means an agreement "for consideration **in the ordinary course of business**" (s1, "rental"). A "consumer" is a person who deals with a supplier "in the ordinary course of the supplier's business" (s1, "consumer" (b)). "Business" means "the continual marketing of any goods or services" (s1).
- **Unsure:** the Act does not define "ordinary course of business". Someone who lets out one spare room once may be outside it. A landlord who lets 9 units, year after year, to the public, with a standard lease, looks like "continual marketing" of a service. I found no court ruling that settles this for small landlords. **The safe course is to assume the CPA applies.** The current lease already says it "affords the Tenant the protection as provided by the Consumer Protection Act", which makes it hard to argue otherwise.
- Other exclusions:
  - The Act does not apply where the **consumer** (the tenant) is a company or other juristic person with assets or yearly turnover of R2 million or more (s5(2)(b); threshold set by GN 294, GG 34181, 1 April 2011, <https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/34181gon294.pdf>). Not relevant where tenants are people.
  - Section 14 alone does not apply "to transactions between juristic persons regardless of their annual turnover or asset value" (s14(1)). Only relevant if both landlord and tenant are companies, trusts, etc.
- If the CPA and RHA clash and both cannot be obeyed, "the provision that extends the greater protection to a consumer prevails" (CPA s2(9)).

## 1. Deposits

All from RHA s5(3), as in force (1999 text as amended in 2007). Text: <https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a50-99.pdf> and the consolidated version at <https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/rha1999171/>. These terms are "deemed" to be in every lease (s5(3)) and "may not be waived by the tenant or the landlord" (s5(4)).

**Amount.** The landlord may ask for a deposit before the tenant moves in. It "may not exceed an amount equivalent to an amount specified in the agreement or otherwise agreed" (s5(3)(c)). The Act sets no cap, such as one or two months' rent.

**Interest-bearing account.** The deposit "must be invested by the landlord in an interest-bearing account with a financial institution". The rate may not be less than that bank's savings rate (s5(3)(d)). "Financial institution" means a bank under the Banks Act (s1). A different rule applies only where the landlord is a registered estate agent (proviso to s5(3)(d)). That does not apply here.

**Interest belongs to the tenant.** The landlord "must ... pay the tenant such interest" (s5(3)(d)). The tenant may ask during the lease for written proof of the interest, and the landlord must give it (s5(3)(d)). Refunds are always of "deposit and interest" (s5(3)(g), (i), (j), (m)).

**Refund deadlines.**

| Situation | Deadline | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing owed | Full deposit plus interest, "without any deduction or set-off, within seven days of expiration of the lease" | s5(3)(i) |
| Deductions made after a joint outgoing inspection | Balance "not later than 14 days of restoration of the dwelling to the landlord" | s5(3)(g) |
| Tenant did not respond to the request for a joint inspection | Landlord must inspect alone within 7 days of expiry; balance "not later than 21 days after expiration of the lease" | s5(3)(k)–(m) |
| Landlord did not do the joint inspection (start or end) | Treated as the landlord accepting the flat is in good repair. No further claim; full deposit plus interest back | s5(3)(j) |

**What may be deducted.** "All amounts for which the tenant is liable under the said lease, including the reasonable cost of repairing damage to the dwelling during the lease period and the cost of replacing lost keys" (s5(3)(g), (l)). Receipts for those costs "must be available to the tenant for inspection" (s5(3)(h), (n)). Also, "any costs in relation to contract of lease shall only be payable by the tenant upon proof of factual expenditure by the landlord" (s5(3)(p), added 2007). The landlord may not charge for fair wear and tear (s4(5)(d)(i)).

**Keeping the whole deposit as a penalty for short notice.** The law does not support this as an automatic rule.

- The landlord may set the deposit off only against amounts the tenant is "liable" for (s5(3)(g)). A penalty the tenant is truly liable for could be one such amount. The question is how much the tenant is liable for.
- If the CPA applies: the penalty for early cancellation must be a "reasonable cancellation penalty" (CPA s14(3)(b)(i)). Reg 5(2) says it "may not exceed a reasonable amount". The factors include the notice the tenant gave, how long the lease was meant to run, and "the reasonable potential for the service provider, acting diligently, to find an alternative consumer" (Reg 5(2)(e), (h), (i)). Reg 5(3) bars any charge "which would have the effect of negating the consumer's right to cancel". Regs: <https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/34180rg9515gon293.pdf>.
- Whether or not the CPA applies: a clause that makes a tenant lose money already paid when they withdraw is a "forfeiture stipulation". It is treated as a penalty (Conventional Penalties Act 15 of 1962 s4). A court "may reduce the penalty" if it is "out of proportion to the prejudice suffered" (s3). The landlord also cannot claim both the penalty and damages for the same breach (s2(1)). Text: <https://www.justice.gov.za/legislation/acts/1962-015.pdf>.
- A tenant who gives 10 days' notice and whose flat is re-let the next week has caused little loss. A rule that they lose R1 500 no matter what is likely to be cut down by a court or the Tribunal.

**Topping up when rent rises.** The RHA links the deposit to "an amount specified in the agreement or otherwise agreed" (s5(3)(c)), not to the rent. Nothing requires a top-up and nothing bans one. A top-up is due only if the lease (or a later agreement) says so.

**Refund = amount paid or current rent?** The amount actually paid, plus interest. The refund duty is to repay "the deposit, together with the accrued interest" (s5(3)(i)). The Act never ties the refund to the rent at the end.

## 2. Aligning all leases to end on 31 December

- **No rule sets a minimum lease length.** The RHA only says the lease must state "the lease period, or, if there is no lease period determined, the notice period" (s5(6)(f)).
- **Maximum:** if the CPA applies, a fixed term may not exceed "24 months from the date of signature by the consumer", unless longer is agreed and the landlord "can show a demonstrable financial benefit to the consumer" (CPA s14(2)(a); Reg 5(1)). Note that the 24 months runs from **signing**, not from moving in.
- So a June–December first lease (about 7 months) and a 10 December to 31 December next-year lease (about 12.7 months) are both lawful.
- Practical points (not legal rules):
  - The current lease heading says "INITIAL PERIOD – 12 MONTH LEASE". If terms vary, the lease must state the real start and end dates.
  - With all leases ending on the same day, the CPA notice window (see 3 below) falls in the same weeks for every tenant, which is easy to plan.
  - A tenant whose fixed term ends on 31 December may leave on that day "without penalty or charge" (CPA s14(2)(b)(i)(aa)). Several tenants could leave at once over the holidays.

## 3. Renewal on changed terms

**May the landlord offer a new lease with new rent and new clauses?** Yes. The landlord has no duty to renew on the old terms unless the lease gives the tenant a right to that. The CPA expects this: the expiry notice must list "any material changes that would apply if the agreement is to be renewed or may otherwise continue" (CPA s14(2)(c)(i)).

**The CPA notice (if the CPA applies).** Section 14(2)(c): "of not more than 80, nor less than 40, business days before the expiry date", the landlord "must notify the consumer in writing or any other recordable form" of:
- the coming expiry date;
- any material changes that would apply after it; and
- the tenant's options under s14(2)(d).

Reg 5 and Annexure B of the CPA Regulations give a form for this notice.

Business days leave out Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays, and are counted by excluding the first day and including the last (CPA s2(6)). By my count (not an official one), for a lease ending **31 December 2026**, the notice must go out **between 7 September and 3 November 2026**. For 31 December 2027 the window is again 7 September to 3 November 2027. (Counted with Heritage Day 24 Sep, Day of Reconciliation 16 Dec, 25 and 26 Dec, and 27 Dec 2027 because 26 Dec falls on a Sunday.) **Offers sent in October fall inside the window.**

**If the tenant does not sign or reply (CPA applies).** On expiry, the lease "will be automatically continued on a month-to-month basis, subject to any material changes of which the supplier has given notice ... unless the consumer expressly (i) directs the supplier to terminate the agreement on the expiry date; or (ii) agrees to a renewal ... for a further fixed term" (s14(2)(d)). So:
- If the notice went out in time and listed the new rent and new clauses, silence means the tenant stays month to month **on the new terms**.
- The landlord cannot treat silence as the tenant leaving.
- **Unsure:** whether a whole new clause (for example a parking clause) counts as a "material change" that binds without a signature. The words suggest it does, but I found no court ruling. It is safer to get a signature.
- **Unsure:** how the landlord ends a month-to-month lease that runs on under s14(2)(d). The CPA only gives the landlord a right to cancel for "material failure" by the tenant (s14(2)(b)(ii)). RHA s5(5) gives either side one month's written notice for a periodic lease. Commentators disagree on how these fit together, and I found no binding judgment.

**If the CPA does not apply.** RHA s5(5): if the tenant stays on with the landlord's consent and there is no new written lease, a periodic lease arises "on the same terms and conditions as the expired lease" (the **old** rent), with at least one month's written notice by either side to end it. The new rent would then need the tenant's agreement, or notice to end followed by a new lease.

**Rent increase notice in the Eastern Cape.** I found no Eastern Cape rule setting a notice period for a rent rise. So the rules are:
- **At renewal:** the CPA's 40 to 80 business days (s14(2)(c)). In practice that is about 8 to 16 weeks, which is longer than Gauteng's 2 months.
- **During a fixed term:** the landlord cannot raise the rent unless the lease allows an escalation. The lease must state any "reasonable escalation" (RHA s5(6)(c)).
- **Month to month under RHA s5(5):** the old terms carry on, so a rise needs agreement, or one month's notice to end the lease followed by a new lease.
- The Tribunal may rule against "exploitative rentals" and may set the rent in a dispute, weighing supply and demand and "a realistic return on investment" (RHA s13(4)(c)(iii), s13(5)).

[Gauteng, does not apply] Gauteng reg 6(4) requires "at least 2 (two) months written notice of an intention to increase rental". A 2-month notice is still a safe habit.

**The current lease's renewal option clashes with the plan.** See "Checks" below.

## 4. Tenant cancellation: notice and penalty

**If the CPA applies**, "despite any provision of the consumer agreement to the contrary", the tenant may cancel a fixed-term lease:
- at expiry, "without penalty or charge" (s14(2)(b)(i)(aa)); or
- "at any other time, by giving the supplier 20 business days' notice in writing or other recorded manner and form" (s14(2)(b)(i)(bb)).

So:
- **A lease may not require more than 20 business days' notice** (about four calendar weeks). A clause asking for, say, two months cannot bind the tenant (s14(2)(b) "despite any provision ... to the contrary"; s51(1)(b)(i) bans terms that take away a CPA right).
- **A 15-day notice clause is valid.** 15 calendar days is shorter than 20 business days, so it gives the tenant more than the Act does. The Act sets the most a tenant can be made to give, not the least a landlord may accept. It does mean the landlord accepts less warning than the law would allow.
- **Penalty.** On early cancellation the tenant still owes what was due up to cancellation (s14(3)(a)). The landlord "may impose a reasonable cancellation penalty" (s14(3)(b)(i)). Reg 5(2) lists what makes it reasonable: amounts still owed, value of the deal so far, agreed length of the lease, the loss suffered, the notice given, the chance of re-letting with diligent effort, and industry practice. Reg 5(3): no charge that would "negat[e] the consumer's right to cancel".
- The penalty is for **cancelling a fixed term early**, even with proper notice. It is not only a fine for short notice. A tenant who gives full notice may still owe a reasonable penalty. A tenant who gives short notice is in breach of the notice rule, and the landlord's claim is still limited to what is reasonable.
- The current clause 2.2.3 says the penalty "shall include, but under no circumstances be limited" to one month's rent. An open-ended penalty conflicts with Reg 5(2)'s "may not exceed a reasonable amount". Clause 2.2.4 (partial refund if a new tenant is found within a month) fits Reg 5(2)(i) well.

**If the CPA does not apply**, the tenant has no statutory right to end a fixed-term lease early. Leaving early would be a breach, and the landlord's remedy would be damages under the common law or a penalty clause limited by the Conventional Penalties Act s3. **Unsure:** I did not check common-law rules on a landlord's duty to limit loss against a primary source.

**Month-to-month lease (after expiry).** RHA s5(5): at least one month's written notice by either party.

## 5. House rules such as a parking-bay clause

- "House Rules" means "the rules in relation to the control, management, administration, use and enjoyment of the rental housing property" (RHA s1).
- "A copy of any House Rules applicable to a dwelling must be attached as an annexure to the lease" (RHA s5(8)). The landlord must make sure this is done (s5(9)).
- A "dwelling" includes "any storeroom, outbuilding, garage or demarcated parking space which is leased as part of the lease" (RHA s1). So a parking bay can be part of what is let.
- Provinces may make unfair-practice rules on house rules (RHA s15(1)(f)(vii)). I found no Eastern Cape rule on this (see "Eastern Cape rules").
- The CPA, if it applies, bars terms that are "unfair, unreasonable or unjust" (s48(1)(a)(ii)). A parking rule that favours one tenant for no reason, or changes in the middle of a lease without consent, could fail that test.
- [Gauteng, does not apply] As an example of what a province can require: Gauteng's rules (reg 11) say a landlord "must make house rules". A house rule "is enforceable against a tenant only if":
  - its purpose is the tenants' and neighbours' convenience, safety, health or welfare, protecting the property, or "a fair distribution of services and facilities";
  - it is "reasonably related" to that purpose;
  - it "applies to all tenants in the premises in a fair manner";
  - it is clear enough to tell the tenant what is expected;
  - it is not meant to dodge the landlord's own duties; and
  - "the tenant has notice of the house rule at the time the tenant enters into the lease agreement" (reg 11(2)(f)).
  A tenant must comply with house rules that meet these tests (reg 7(2)(i)).
- **Answer for the Eastern Cape:** a parking-bay rule (who may park where, one bay per unit, no parking in front of gates) is part of the contract, and so binding, if it is in the lease or attached to it when the tenant signs (RHA s5(8)). It should be fair and apply the same way to everyone (CPA s48). **A rule added in the middle of a lease binds only if the tenant agrees.** A landlord cannot change a contract alone. The yearly renewal is the right time to add it, with a signature. The Gauteng tests above are a sound checklist even though they do not bind here.
- **Unsure:** whether Eastern Cape rules exist that I could not find.

## 6. Required contents and formalities (RHA as in force)

**Writing.** A lease "need not be in writing", but "a landlord must, if requested thereto by a tenant, reduce the lease to writing" (s5(1)–(2)). The Formalities in Respect of Leases of Land Act does not apply (s5(1)). (If the 2014 Act starts, writing becomes compulsory.)

**Required details in a written lease** (s5(6)):
- (a) names of tenant and landlord and their South African addresses for formal notices;
- (b) description of the dwelling;
- (c) rent and "reasonable escalation, if any";
- (d) how often rent is paid, if not monthly;
- (e) the deposit amount;
- (f) the lease period, or the notice period if there is no fixed period;
- (g) the duties of both sides, which "must not detract from" s5(3) or the unfair-practice rules;
- (h) the rent and any other charges payable on top of it.

**Annexures.**
- The list of defects found at the move-in inspection "must be attached as an annexure to the lease" (s5(7)).
- House rules must be attached (s5(8)).
- The landlord must make sure (6), (7) and (8) are done (s5(9)).

**Joint inspections.**
- Before move-in, tenant and landlord "must jointly ... inspect the dwelling" to record defects (s5(3)(e)).
- At the end, they "must arrange a joint inspection ... within a period of three days prior to such expiration" (s5(3)(f)).
- If the landlord does not inspect with the tenant, the landlord loses any claim and must refund everything (s5(3)(j)).

**Receipts.** The landlord "must furnish the tenant with a written receipt for all payments". It must be dated and show the address, what the payment was for (rent, arrears, deposit or other) and the period covered (s5(3)(a)–(b)). A Tribunal can excuse a landlord from the details "in exceptional cases" (s5(3)(b) proviso). [Gauteng, does not apply] Gauteng's rules treat a bank deposit slip as the receipt where the landlord names the account (reg 12(2)). I found no such Eastern Cape rule, so **in the Eastern Cape the landlord should issue a receipt for every payment**, even payments by EFT. The app can do this.

**Other points.**
- The landlord may end the lease only "on grounds that do not constitute an unfair practice and are specified in the lease" (s4(5)(c)).
- The landlord may take back the property only "having first obtained an order of court" (s4(5)(d)(ii)).
- Inspections during the lease need "reasonable notice" (s4(2)).
- If the tenant leaves early without notice, the lease is treated as ending when the landlord found out, and the landlord keeps its claims for breach (s5(3)(o)).
- If the CPA applies, a written consumer agreement "applies irrespective of whether or not the consumer signs", and the landlord must give the tenant a free copy (CPA s50(2)). [Gauteng, does not apply] Gauteng reg 4 also gives an unsigned lease full effect once the tenant moves in and pays rent.

## 7. Electronic signatures

ECTA text: <https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a25-02.pdf>.

- **Writing:** "A requirement in law that a document or information must be in writing is met if the document or information is (a) in the form of a data message; and (b) accessible in a manner usable for subsequent reference" (ECTA s12). A lease saved as a PDF or web record meets this.
- **Signature required by law** → only an "advanced electronic signature" will do (s13(1)). That is a signature from a process accredited by the state authority (s1, s37).
- **Signature required by the parties** → it is enough if "(a) a method is used to identify the person and to indicate the person's approval of the information communicated; and (b) ... the method was as reliable as was appropriate for the purposes" (s13(3)).
- "Electronic signature" means "data attached to, incorporated in, or logically associated with other data and which is intended by the user to serve as a signature" (s1).
- **Excluded deals:** the sale of land, and leases of land for **more than 20 years** under the Alienation of Land Act (s4(4), Schedule 2). An ordinary residential lease is not on the list.
- **No law requires a residential lease to be signed.** The RHA does not even require writing unless the tenant asks (s5(1)–(2)), and the Formalities in Respect of Leases of Land Act is excluded (s5(1)). So s13(1) (advanced signature) does not bite. Any signature need comes from the lease itself, which brings in s13(3).
- *Spring Forest Trading 599 CC v Wilberry (Pty) Ltd* [2014] ZASCA 178; 2015 (2) SA 118 (SCA), <https://www.saflii.org/za/cases/ZASCA/2014/178.html>:
  - The Supreme Court of Appeal held that s13(1) applies only where a statute requires a signature, not where the parties agree to one (paras [18]–[22]).
  - Under s13(3), names typed at the foot of emails were valid signatures (para [28]).
  - The court said the approach to signatures is "pragmatic, not formalistic" (para [26]).
- **Answer:** a signature drawn on a web page, tied to the lease text, is very likely valid under s13(3). How reliable is "appropriate" depends on the facts. It helps to record the signer's name and ID number, email or phone, a time stamp, IP address, and a copy of the exact text signed, and to email the tenant a copy. **Unsure:** I found no judgment on a drawn web signature on a lease specifically. The conclusion comes from applying *Spring Forest* and s13(3).

## Eastern Cape rules

The property is in the Eastern Cape.

**Result: I could not find any Eastern Cape unfair-practice rules made under RHA s15(1)(f) in a primary source.** This does not prove none exist.

Where I looked (26 September 2026):
- **Eastern Cape Department of Human Settlements website.** The Tribunal page (<https://www.ecdhs.gov.za/index.php/tribunal/>) describes the Tribunal but links no rules. The site's documents library lists the Rental Housing Act 50 of 1999, the "Rental Housing Bill 2006" and Tribunal complaint forms, but no rules. A site search for "regulations", "unfair" and "tribunal" found only two 2021 event entries titled "RENTAL HOUSING REGULATIONS (Public Information Session)", with no text or files attached.
- **Eastern Cape Provincial Gazettes** (full-text search on gazettes.africa; files from archive.opengazettes.org.za). The only Rental Housing Act notices I found were calls for Tribunal members:
  - Provincial Gazette 2169, 31 July 2009, notice 45, <https://archive.opengazettes.org.za/archive/ZA-EC/2009/provincial-gazette-ZA-EC-vol-16-no-2169-dated-2009-07-31.pdf>;
  - Provincial Gazette 2581, 9 June 2011, notice 25, <https://archive.opengazettes.org.za/archive/ZA-EC/2011/provincial-gazette-ZA-EC-vol-18-no-2581-dated-2011-06-09.pdf>.
  A search for "unfair practices" with "rental housing" matched gazettes from Gauteng, Free State, North West and KwaZulu-Natal (I did not open those), but none from the Eastern Cape.
- **Limits of the search:** gazettes.africa blocks automated reading, so I could open only some Eastern Cape hits. The opengazettes archive stops in 2021. Rules gazetted but never scanned, or gazetted after 2021, could be missed.
- **National rules:** the national drafts of 2008, 2021 (GN 262, GG 44333, <https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202103/44333gon262.pdf>) and 2022 (GN 1913, GG 46063) are all marked as drafts for comment. I found no final national rules.

**What this means for each point asked:**

1. **Notice before a rent rise.** No provincial rule found. The CPA 40–80 business-day notice at renewal is the test (s14(2)(c)). During a fixed term, a rise needs an escalation clause (RHA s5(6)(c)). See section 3.
2. **House rules (parking).** No provincial rule found. RHA s5(8) (attach to lease) and CPA s48 (fair terms) apply. A rule binds if it is part of the signed lease. A new rule in the middle of a lease needs the tenant's consent. See section 5.
3. **Deposits, refunds, inspections.** No provincial rule found. RHA s5(3) governs in full: interest-bearing account, 7/14/21-day refunds, joint inspections, receipts. See sections 1 and 6.
4. **Expiry, renewal, month to month.** No provincial rule found. CPA s14(2)(c)–(d) and RHA s5(5) apply. See section 3.
5. **Other effects on the plans.** Without provincial rules, the Tribunal still acts:
   - "Unfair practice" includes "any act or omission by a landlord or tenant in contravention of this Act" (RHA s1, as amended in 2007). A breach of the deposit rules in s5(3) is therefore an unfair practice the Tribunal can hear.
   - The Tribunal "may ... make any other ruling that is just and fair to terminate any unfair practice", including on "exploitative rentals" (s13(4)(c)). It may set the rent in a dispute (s13(5)).
   - A Tribunal ruling "is deemed to be an order of a magistrate's court" (s13(13), as amended in 2007).
   - So the deposit-forfeit plan and a sharp rent rise could both be challenged there at no cost to the tenant.

**Where a landlord can get the rules, if they exist.** Ask the Eastern Cape Rental Housing Tribunal whether the province has unfair-practice rules, and for a copy with the gazette number and date:
- Secretariat: tel 086 000 0039, fax 043 711 9797/86, email info@ecdhs.gov.za (from <https://www.ecdhs.gov.za/index.php/tribunal/>).
- Office: 31–33 Phillip Frame Road, Waverly Park, Chislehurst, East London; tel 043 711 9622 (from the national Department of Human Settlements leaflet, <https://www.dhs.gov.za/sites/default/files/publications/RENTAL%20HOUSING%20TRIBUNAL2.pdf>, which may be out of date).

The Government Printing Works (<https://www.gpwonline.co.za>) sells back copies of Provincial Gazettes.

**Gauteng material in this note does not apply to this property.** It is marked "[Gauteng, does not apply]" and kept only as an example of what provincial rules can say.

## Checks against the current lease

Lease read: `public/LEASE AGREEMENT updated.01.pdf`. The landlord's plan was supplied with the brief.

| Item | Lease or plan | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cl 2.1 heading "12 MONTH LEASE" | Fixed 12-month first term | **Conflicts with the plan** (not with the law) | If first terms run to 31 Dec, the lease must state the real dates (RHA s5(6)(f)). Rename to "Initial period" and fill in start and end dates. |
| Cl 2.1.2 option to renew for 12 months, used in writing 3 months before expiry | Tenant's right | **Conflicts with the plan** | If a tenant uses the option, cl 2.1.3 binds the landlord to renew "on the same terms", which means no new rent and no new parking clause. Remove the option, or say renewal is at the landlord's offered terms. |
| Cl 2.1.3 renewal "on the same terms" | Same terms | **Conflicts with the plan**, and clashes with cl 2.1.4 | Cl 2.1.4 expects the rent to rise, which "same terms" does not allow. |
| Cl 2.1.4 deposit top-up in proportion to rent | Tenant tops up | **Conflicts with the plan**; lawful either way | A top-up is a contract matter (RHA s5(3)(c)). If the landlord will not top up, delete the clause so the lease matches practice. |
| Cl 2.1.5 one option period only | | Mostly moot under the CPA | Under CPA s14(2)(d) the lease rolls on month to month after expiry anyway. |
| Plan: all leases end 31 Dec | | **Law supports it** | No minimum term; max 24 months from signing (Reg 5(1)). |
| Plan: renewal offers each October with new rent | | **Law supports it, with conditions** | Must be sent between about 7 Sep and 3 Nov for a 31 Dec expiry (CPA s14(2)(c)). Must list the changes and the tenant's options (Reg annexure B form). No Eastern Cape rule on rent-rise notice found, so the CPA window is the test. Sending by 31 Oct also gives 2 months' warning, which is a safe habit. |
| Plan: no reply deadline | | **Unclear, leans against the plan** | Under the CPA, silence means month to month on the new terms (s14(2)(d)), so no deadline is needed for that. But if the CPA does not apply, a tenant who stays on is on the **old** terms (RHA s5(5)). A reply date, and a statement of what happens if there is no reply, avoids the doubt. |
| Cl 2.2.2 at least 15 days' written notice | | **Law supports it** | Shorter than the CPA's 20 business days, so better for the tenant (s14(2)(b)(i)(bb)). |
| Cl 2.2.2 / 2.2.3.1 one month's rent as the penalty | | **Unclear to risky** | One month's rent may be reasonable in many cases, but it must be judged against Reg 5(2). "Under no circumstances be limited to" (cl 2.2.3) conflicts with Reg 5(2)–(3). Cl 2.2.4 (partial refund if re-let) helps. |
| Plan: tenant giving under 15 days' notice loses the whole deposit | | **Conflicts with the law** | A deposit may be set off only against amounts truly owed (RHA s5(3)(g)). A forfeit is a penalty that a court can cut to the real loss (Conventional Penalties Act s3–4). A CPA penalty must be reasonable and must not negate the right to cancel (Reg 5(2)–(3)). Also, a deposit is not always one month's rent. Instead: deduct a reasonable penalty backed by real loss (unpaid days, time empty), show the working, refund the rest on time. |
| Plan: no top-up; refund what the tenant paid | | **Law supports it**, but interest must be added | Refund is deposit plus interest earned (RHA s5(3)(d), (i)). |
| Cl 3.1.1 rent R1 500 or R3 000 | | **Law supports it** | Lease must state the rent (s5(6)(c)). The app should fill in the actual figure for the actual unit, not "or". |
| Cl 3.2.1 deposit R1 500 or R3 000 | | **Law supports it** | Any agreed amount (s5(3)(c)); state the actual figure (s5(6)(e)). Keep it in an interest-bearing bank account (s5(3)(d)). |
| Cl 3.2.2 outgoing inspection "on the afternoon of the last day" | | **Mostly fits** | The Act says "within a period of three days prior to such expiration" (s5(3)(f)). The last day falls in that window. |
| Cl 3.2.5 balance refunded "within fourteen (14) days of the repairs been attended to" | | **Conflicts with the law** | The Act says 14 days "of restoration of the dwelling to the landlord" (s5(3)(g)), not from when repairs are done. The statutory term wins (s5(4)). |
| Cl 3.2.3, 3.2.4, 3.2.6, 3.2.7 | | **Law supports it** | These copy s5(3)(k), (j), (h), (i). |
| No move-in inspection clause, only a defects checklist | | **Check** | The joint move-in inspection is required (s5(3)(e)), and the list must be attached (s5(7)). If it is skipped, the landlord loses claims for damage (s5(3)(j)). The app should record it. |
| House rules / parking clause | | **Law supports it if attached at signing** | RHA s5(8); CPA s48. No Eastern Cape rule found. Adding it in the middle of a lease needs the tenant's consent. |
| Rent paid by EFT with no receipt | | **Check** | RHA s5(3)(a)–(b) requires a written receipt for every payment. No Eastern Cape rule treats a bank slip as a receipt. The app should issue receipts. |

**Other points spotted while reading the lease** (outside the brief; not fully researched):

- Cl 8.4 charges 3% a month interest on late payments. [Gauteng, does not apply] Gauteng reg 3(3)(a) bars late-payment penalties other than interest. I found no Eastern Cape rule on this. I did not check whether 3% a month is lawful under other laws. **Unsure.**
- Cl 5.3 imposes a fixed "fine of R1500" for electricity theft. That is a penalty clause, subject to the Conventional Penalties Act s3.
- Cl 3.1.4 lets the landlord have goods "attached and removed". The RHA bars seizing a tenant's possessions without a Tribunal ruling or court order (s4(3)(c)).
